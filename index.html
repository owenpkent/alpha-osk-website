The smartest keyboard you’ll never touch.

Alpha-OSK is an on-screen keyboard that types into whatever app you are already using. Click the keys with a mouse, a trackball, a head pointer, or anything else that moves a cursor. As you go, it learns your words and offers the rest of them back, so a long word costs three clicks instead of thirteen.

How it works

Three clicks to your first sentence

There is no setup, no account, and no training period you have to sit through. Install it, click into the app you want to type into, and start.

Click into any app

The keyboard floats above your other windows but never takes focus away from them. Whatever you clicked last is where your keystrokes land: a browser, an editor, a terminal, a chat window, a game, even a remote desktop session.

Click letters, get keystrokes

Each key is delivered to the operating system exactly as a physical keypress would be. Modifiers are sticky, so Ctrl+Alt+Del is three separate clicks rather than a chord you have to hold. Right-click a modifier to lock it down.

Take the words it offers

Prediction pills appear above the keys after a few characters. Click one and the rest of the word is typed for you, plus a space. Right-click a pill to teach the keyboard that you want more of that word, less of it, or none of it ever again.

Prediction

It finishes your words, and it forgives your aim

Every click on this keyboard costs real effort, so the engine is built to remove them. It merges a word-frequency model of your own vocabulary with a spatial model of the keys around the one you actually hit.

That second half is what matters if your pointer is not precise. Type hwllo and hello is still the first suggestion, because the engine searches the dictionary allowing for a slipped click, a missed one, a doubled one, or two in the wrong order. It also notices where inside each key you tend to land and quietly corrects for it.

It learns numbers too: your phone number, your zip, your house number, your email domain. Long digit runs are refused outright, so a card number or a Social Security number can never be stored.

This demo runs a real prediction pass in your browser on every keypress, so it needs JavaScript to do anything. The screenshots above show the same keyboard, and the download below is unaffected.

Click the keys, or type here

Try typing hwllo, hitting W instead of the E you meant. The suggestions still find hello. This runs the same approach the application does, on the same wordlist, in your browser: nothing you type here is sent anywhere.

Features

Everything a working day needs

This is not a demo or a proof of concept. It is the keyboard its author uses to write every line of it.

Layouts that fit your screen

QWERTY, Dvorak and Colemak, plus a Compact View that folds the whole board into a dense 13×4 grid with symbols one tap away. Turn the function row, navigation cluster and numpad on or off independently.

Voice dictation, on a toggle

Click the microphone, speak, click again. There is nothing to hold down, which matters if a sustained press is exactly the gesture you cannot make. It stops itself after a few seconds of silence. Off by default; bring your own Deepgram key.

A model you can see

A dashboard shows what the keyboard has learned: a word cloud of your vocabulary, a graph of which words follow which, and lifetime counts of the keystrokes and hours prediction has saved you. Click any word to drill into its context.

Snippets for what you retype

Your address, your email, a canned reply: one tap puts it on the clipboard. The keyboard can spot an email, phone number or address as you type it and offer to save it, on your machine only, and never while learning is paused.

Passwords pause it automatically

When focus lands on a password field, learning stops, predictions stop, and nothing you type is recorded. Detected through Windows UI Automation and Linux AT-SPI, with a manual switch in the title bar you can flip at any time.

Take your model with you

Everything the keyboard has learned exports to a single zip file you control: your vocabulary, your stats, your snippets, your imported dictionaries. Import it on another machine and carry on mid-sentence. No restart needed.

Tuned for slow, imprecise input

Repeat delay and repeat interval are yours to set. Targets are large. A click held a beat too long is never reinterpreted as a different gesture. Nothing in the interface requires a drag, a hold, or a double-click.

Updates that install themselves

The Windows build checks for releases, verifies the signature and the version of what it downloads before running it, and installs in place. You can turn the check off entirely in settings.

Privacy

A keyboard sees everything. This one keeps it.

Prediction runs on your machine. There is no model upload, no cloud round-trip, and no large language model anywhere in the product. The claims below are the ones the code actually makes, including the one exception.

Learning is local

Your vocabulary, your word pairs, your saved numbers and your snippets are files in your own config directory. Nothing about them is transmitted.

No LLM, no GPU

The engine is an n-gram model merged with a spatial error model. It is a few megabytes of JSON and some arithmetic, which is why it runs in milliseconds on any machine.

Password fields stop it

Learning pauses automatically when focus reaches a password field, and there is a manual switch in the title bar that overrides the detection either way.

Sensitive numbers are refused

The number learner rejects long digit runs outright, so card numbers and Social Security numbers are never stored. Everything it did learn is listed in the dashboard, and removable one at a time.

The log never holds your typing

The diagnostic log records errors and crashes so you have something to attach to a bug report. It records lengths and outcomes, never characters.

Dictation is the exception

If you switch on dictation and supply your own Deepgram key, audio goes to Deepgram while the microphone is open, and at no other time. It is off by default, inert without a key, never written to disk, and a password field cancels a run outright.

Releases are published on GitHub. The Windows installer is signed by OK Studio Inc. and updates itself; the Linux build is an AppImage you can run from anywhere.

Download AppImage Latest release
macOS

In progress. The key synthesiser, window behaviour and password detection are written; code signing and notarisation are not done yet.

Not released yet

Windows may show a blue “Windows protected your PC” screen on first install. That is normal for newer applications and does not mean anything is wrong. Click More info, check that the publisher reads OK Studio Inc., then click Run anyway. The prompt stops appearing on its own as more people install.

Prefer to build it yourself? Clone the repository and run python run.py. It creates a virtual environment, installs the dependencies, and launches the keyboard.

Questions

Frequently asked

Who is this for?

Anyone who cannot comfortably use a physical keyboard but can move a cursor. That includes people with muscular dystrophy, ALS, spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, arthritis and repetitive strain injury, and people using a head pointer, an eye tracker, a trackball, a joystick or a switch-access setup. It is also useful on a touchscreen or a kiosk where no keyboard is attached.

Does it work with my screen reader or my other assistive software?

It types into whatever application has focus, so anything that accepts keyboard input accepts Alpha-OSK. It deliberately never takes focus itself, which is what keeps it out of the way of the tools you already run. It reads the pointer state without capturing it, so dwell-click and switch-access utilities keep working alongside it.

My keystrokes are not appearing in VS Code, a game, or a remote desktop.

Open Settings → Smart Typing → Input and set Compatibility Mode to “Always On”. Auto-detect is on by default and covers VS Code, the JetBrains IDEs, TeamViewer, RDP, VNC and AnyDesk, but anything else may need the manual override. Games are handled separately: they read the keyboard by polling, so the keyboard holds each key down briefly rather than tapping it instantly.

The predictions are wrong, or capitalised oddly.

Right-click any pill. “Show less” downweights it, “Remove” suppresses it entirely, and “Edit” lets you fix the capitalisation, for example iphone to iPhone. A suppressed word comes back on its own after you type it manually three times, so a regrettable suppression is never permanent.

Where does my data live, and how do I move it?

On Windows, %APPDATA%\alpha-osk\. On Linux, ~/.config/alpha-osk/. Settings → Data & Privacy → Data Backup writes all of it to a single zip file: your model, your lifetime stats, your snippets and any imported dictionaries. Importing on a new machine restores everything without a restart, and writes a rescue copy of what was there before, so a mistaken import is one click to undo.

Does any of this send my typing to a server?

Your typing, no. Your voice, only if you switch on dictation and supply your own Deepgram API key, and only while the microphone is open.

There is an opt-in anonymous statistics client in the build, but its endpoint is not deployed, so it does nothing at all today. If it is ever turned on, opting in would send nine integer counters per week, such as how many keystrokes you have typed. Never content, never word frequencies, never your IP or hostname.

Can I add my own vocabulary?

Yes. Settings → Your Language Model → Vocabulary Packs → Import Custom Pack. A pack is a folder containing dictionary.txt, one word per line, and optionally files of word pairs and triples. No packs ship built in, because a three-hundred-word list is beaten by your own typing within a week.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It is MIT licensed, free for personal and commercial use, with no account, no subscription and no upsell. If you use dictation you pay Deepgram directly for your own API usage; that is the only money involved anywhere, and it is optional.

How do I report a bug?

Open an issue on the GitHub repository. Attach the diagnostic log: Settings → Data & Privacy → Diagnostics has an Open Log Folder button. The log records errors and crashes and never records what you type. For security issues, follow the disclosure process in SECURITY.md rather than opening a public issue.

Get your keyboard back.

Free, open source, and built by somebody who depends on it every day. If it does not work for you, the issue tracker is open and the author reads it.
